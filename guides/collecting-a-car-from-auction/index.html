Collecting a car from auction

Winning the bid is the easy part. This checklist gets the car out of the yard before storage charges start adding up.

Before you book transport

  • Pay in full. Most sites will not release a car until payment has cleared.
  • Check the deadline. Auction sites usually allow only a few days of free storage after the sale, then charge per day. The exact terms are in your buyer agreement.
  • Get the release. Some sites issue a release or collection note by email, some need a booked slot.

What the transporter needs

  • Site name and address, and the lot or stock number.
  • Your buyer or member number, and any collection PIN.
  • The vehicle’s condition as listed: runner, non-runner, keys or no keys, damage.
  • The delivery address and a phone number at that end.

Salvage and non-runners

Cat N and Cat S cars often have locked wheels, flat tyres or damage that stops them rolling. Tell us before we send a truck so the driver brings skates and uses the winch.

Ready to book? See auction collection.

Damaged and Cat N or Cat S cars

Many salvage auction cars are insurance write-offs. A Cat N car has non-structural damage; a Cat S car has structural damage. Both can be repaired and put back on the road, but they usually can’t be driven away from the auction. Some have airbag lights on, missing keys or flat tyres. Tell us what the auction listing says, and send us the photos, so the driver brings the right kit.

BCA Peterborough

BCA’s Peterborough site, close to the A1(M), is a regular collection point for us, alongside Copart at Wisbech. Trade buyers and private buyers both use it. If you’ve bought a car there, have the buyer paperwork ready and book collection quickly. See auction collection.
